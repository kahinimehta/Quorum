The proposal

Parkinson's disease pipeline — NIH R01 research proposal

Central hypothesis. We hypothesize that, in Parkinson's disease patients, a jointly measured panel spanning lysosomal-glucocerebrosidase genotype/activity, gut-microbiome and metabolomic features, and peripheral monocyte/exosome profiles will define subgroups that differ in their candidacy for mechanism-specific disease-modifying interventions; we will test whether such multi-tissue stratification distinguishes patients in whom a lysosomal-targeting agent engages its target without clinical benefit from those flagged by GBA pre-clinical risk markers, gut-axis features, and peripheral immune signatures.
Significance serves F1

We will investigate whether an integrated, multi-tissue biomarker framework can define which Parkinson's disease patients are appropriate candidates for mechanism-specific disease-modifying trials. This question is worth investigating because a recent randomized lysosomal-targeting trial (ambroxol) demonstrated biochemical target engagement — increased beta-glucocerebrosidase levels — yet showed no group differences on clinical outcomes, raising the unresolved question of whether unstratified enrollment, rather than the lysosomal target, limits trial success; because GBA mutations represent the single largest genetic risk factor and offer a window for identifying PD at a pre-clinical stage; because the gut and its microbiome are proposed sites for early diagnosis and disease-modifying intervention whose value in patient selection remains untested; and because peripheral monocyte and exosome biology has been described as a candidate route of periphery-to-brain communication that has not been evaluated as a stratification tool. Determining whether these axes can be jointly measured to enrich trial populations would address an unmet need in the PD therapeutic pipeline, where slowing progression through validated biomarkers and targeted therapies is a stated research priority.

Innovation serves F1

The departure from current practice is to treat patient stratification, not target identity, as the primary experimental variable in the PD disease-modification pipeline: rather than testing a single mechanism in an unselected cohort, we will build a unified multi-tissue assay that co-measures lysosomal-glucocerebrosidase status, gut-microbiome/metabolomic features, and peripheral monocyte/exosome profiles in the same participants — a cross-axis stratification instrument that has not previously been assembled, directly addressing the field's call to slow progression via validated biomarkers and targeted therapies.

AIM-1
Hypothesis

We will investigate whether a single, harmonized multi-tissue assay can co-measure GBA genotype/glucocerebrosidase (GCase) enzymatic activity, gut-microbiome/plasma-metabolomic features, and peripheral monocyte/exosome profiles in the same prospectively enrolled PD participants, and we will examine whether these three domains can be jointly captured at analytic quality sufficient to define candidate cross-tissue feature clusters that differentiate PD cases from controls. This is an exploratory feasibility/biomarker-establishment objective; no causal or progression-modifying claim is made.

Approach

Design: prospective single-protocol observational cohort. We will enroll n=240 (160 PD meeting MDS clinical criteria + 80 age/sex-matched controls), powered (alpha=0.05, two-sided, 80%) to detect a moderate standardized between-group difference (Cohen's d≈0.45) in any single continuous domain readout with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control across features. At one baseline visit we collect: (1) blood for GBA targeted sequencing of common/known pathogenic variants plus a dried-blood-spot/leukocyte GCase activity fluorometric assay (4-MU substrate), run in duplicate with inter-plate QC standards; (2) stool for shotgun metagenomic sequencing and paired plasma for untargeted LC-MS metabolomics, including a caffeine-metabolite targeted panel given prior PD signal; (3) peripheral blood for flow-cytometric/CyTOF monocyte subset immunophenotyping (classical/intermediate/non-classical, phagocytic markers) and plasma exosome isolation (size-exclusion + nanoparticle tracking) with exosomal alpha-synuclein and cargo profiling. The unifying deliverable is a locked SOP for synchronized aliquoting, batch-randomized processing, and a shared sample-ID schema linking all three domains per participant. Analysis: per-domain QC and normalization, then multi-omic integration (e.g., MOFA/sparse multiblock factor analysis) to derive cross-tissue feature clusters; case-control discrimination assessed by penalized regression with nested cross-validation (report AUC with 95% CI, no over-fit claims). Expertise/resources assumed (F3): a CLIA-adjacent genetics lab for GBA/GCase, a metagenomics core, an LC-MS metabolomics core, a CyTOF/flow core, exosome-isolation capability, a movement-disorders clinic for prospective enrollment, and a biostatistician/multi-omics analyst — capacities consistent with the multi-tissue proteomic/genomic and gut-microbiota programs cited.

Expected outcomes

Primary feasibility outcome: completed tri-domain datasets for ≥90% of enrolled participants with pre-specified QC pass rates (GCase assay CV <15%; metagenomic reads ≥10M/sample; metabolomic internal-standard recovery within tolerance; monocyte panels with viable events ≥50,000). We expect to deliver a locked, reproducible co-measurement SOP and an integrated per-participant feature matrix. Exploratory analytic outcome: identification of ≥1 candidate cross-tissue feature cluster distinguishing PD from controls (target discrimination AUC reported with 95% CI; FDR<0.10 for any individual feature differences). Because evidence here is unscored, results will be framed as hypothesis-generating candidate signals and effect-size estimates to power confirmatory aims, not as established associations.

AIM-2
Hypothesis

We will investigate whether integrating cross-axis biomarkers — gut-metabolomic features (e.g., caffeine and its downstream metabolites), 16S/shotgun microbiome composition, lysosomal/glucocerebrosidase-pathway readouts, and peripheral-immune (monocyte phenotype, exosomal markers) measures — can partition a PD cohort into reproducible, internally validated patient subgroups that are distinguishable from controls and from one another. Whether any such subgroups exist, whether they are stable across analytical methods, and whether they bear any relationship to clinical features are entirely open questions to be determined by this work; no relationship between any single axis and clinical severity is assumed or asserted at the outset.

Approach

Cross-sectional discovery + replication design. We will enroll a discovery cohort (n≈200 PD, n≈80 controls; powered to detect ≥2 subgroups each ≥15% prevalence with silhouette stability) and an independent replication cohort (n≈150 PD). Per participant we will collect: (i) fasting plasma untargeted LC-MS/MS metabolomics with targeted quantification of caffeine and metabolites (paraxanthine, theobromine, theophylline); (ii) stool 16S rRNA and shotgun metagenomic sequencing; (iii) lysosomal/GBA-axis readouts — GBA genotyping plus blood β-glucocerebrosidase enzyme activity and glucosylsphingosine; (iv) peripheral-immune profiling — flow-cytometry monocyte subset composition and plasma exosome-associated markers. Analysis: features are batch-corrected, scaled, and integrated via similarity network fusion / multi-omic factor analysis followed by consensus clustering; cluster number is selected by consensus and silhouette criteria, then frozen and applied to the replication cohort. Reproducibility is the primary endpoint, quantified by adjusted Rand index between discovery and replication assignments and by bootstrap cluster-stability. Subgroups will be described against clinical metadata (MDS-UPDRS) descriptively only, with no causal/association inference. Multiple-comparison control by Benjamini-Hochberg (FDR<0.05). Expertise/resources assumed (F3): metabolomics core with validated caffeine-metabolite assay, microbiome sequencing and bioinformatics pipeline, a GBA genotyping/enzymology lab, flow-cytometry/exosome immunology capacity, a deeply phenotyped PD biobank with two independent cohorts, and a statistician experienced in multi-omic integration.

Expected outcomes

We expect to derive ≥2 candidate multi-axis subgroups in the discovery cohort that survive replication with adjusted Rand index ≥0.4 and bootstrap cluster-stability ≥0.7, with at least one subgroup enriched for altered caffeine-metabolite profiles and/or low β-glucocerebrosidase/elevated glucosylsphingosine, and at least one distinguished by monocyte/exosomal immune features. As exploratory output we will report subgroup-defining feature loadings (effect sizes with 95% CIs, FDR<0.05) and descriptive clinical profiles. A null result — features failing to produce reproducible partitions — is also informative and will be reported.

AIM-3
Hypothesis

We will investigate whether the multi-tissue stratification framework developed in Aims 1–2 can define a priori PD subgroups — primarily GBA-pathway/lysosomal-burden-high vs. -low strata — and, if such strata are identified, whether stratum membership and the magnitude of target engagement by a lysosomal-targeting agent (ambroxol, a β-glucocerebrosidase chaperone) co-vary in any systematic way. Whether strata of this kind can be identified, whether stratum membership relates to target engagement in any direction or magnitude, and whether any such relationship could inform enrichment strategies for future mechanism-specific trials are all open questions this aim is designed to investigate; no directional relationship between stratum membership and target engagement is assumed at the outset.

Approach

Design: prospective, single-center pharmacodynamic (engagement-focused) study, not a powered efficacy trial. Participants (n≈90, target ~30 per stratum) drawn from the Aim 1–2 cohort are classified by the multi-tissue framework into lysosomal/GBA-pathway strata (high vs. intermediate vs. low) using the genotype, peripheral-tissue, and proteomic features developed earlier; investigators delivering drug and running assays are blinded to stratum. All participants receive high-dose oral ambroxol over a 24-week open-label engagement window. Primary readout: change in β-glucocerebrosidase enzymatic activity and protein level in accessible compartments (peripheral blood mononuclear cells/monocytes and CSF), measured at baseline, 12, and 24 weeks by validated fluorometric activity assay and immunoassay, mirroring the engagement endpoint used in. Secondary pharmacodynamic readouts: CSF/plasma glucosylceramide and glucosylsphingosine substrate levels by LC-MS/MS and exploratory α-synuclein species. Analysis: linear mixed-effects models of biomarker trajectory with stratum × time interaction as the prespecified test of differential engagement; effect estimates with 95% CIs reported; correction for multiple compartments via false-discovery rate. Feasibility leverages established multi-tissue proteomic/genomic infrastructure, lysosomal-function expertise, and Gaucher/GBA clinical and assay expertise. F3 — this approach assumes access to: a deeply phenotyped multi-tissue PD cohort with banked CSF/PBMCs and longitudinal follow-up; a validated, reproducible β-glucocerebrosidase activity/immunoassay pipeline and LC-MS/MS sphingolipid platform; clinical-trial regulatory, pharmacy, and blinding infrastructure for an investigational disease-modifying agent; and statistical/biostatistics support for mixed-effects modeling of biomarker trajectories.

Expected outcomes

If the framework captures lysosomal-pathway biology, we expect a detectable stratum × time interaction, with the lysosomal-burden-high stratum showing the largest β-glucocerebrosidase activity increase from baseline (target effect of interest: ≥30–50% relative increase in the high stratum vs. a smaller change in the low stratum), with the interaction term reaching p<0.05 (FDR-adjusted). Substrate readouts (glucosylceramide/glucosylsphingosine) would be expected to move directionally consistent with enzyme engagement in the high stratum. A null or uniform engagement across strata is an equally informative exploratory result, indicating the stratification does not partition engagement and should not be used for enrichment. Deliverable: an estimated engagement effect size per stratum to power future mechanism-specific trials. Consistent with the unscored, exploratory evidence base and the absence of clinical benefit in, no claim of clinical disease modification is made.
